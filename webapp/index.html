    The app checks every block it uses itself: its hash, its difficulty and its proof-of-work seal, from the start this build pins for the network, and keeps the chain with the most work. A chain that fails the check shows no confirmations.

    About

    Vanish commits SPECTER from your wallet to a new Phantom Key of 24 words. The key is the only authority over the coin: write it down before anything is sent. Each coin's commit carries its own random salt, and the 24 words hold a 256-bit secret, so the key stays strong even against a quantum computer. The commit comes from the wallet you connect, which holds your account's quantum-safe key (ML-DSA-44) and asks you to approve it; a Phantom Key is not your wallet's backup, and the two are kept apart.

    Summon opens a pasted Phantom Key, finds its coin and sends any amount of it to your connected wallet. The rest, less the network fee, stays under the same key. A key whose check fails is refused before anything is derived from it; a wrong word is named by its position, never corrected.

    No key or wallet seed is stored: not in the browser, not in a file, not on a server. This page never sees your wallet's seed or account key, and it does not remember the connection: connect again after a reload. Proofs are made in this browser in a background worker; there is no proving key to download. Every request goes to the node shown under Network (your own Specter node, a public test node when none answers on this computer, or the endpoint you set), and none names your key or your coin; your wallet is asked only for its account and to sign the commit.

    Amounts, timing and your network address can still link a reveal to its commit, and one block holding a transaction is neither final settlement nor privacy.
