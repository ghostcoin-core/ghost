# Recursion layers: results

The stage 2 design (stage2-design.md, "Aggregation cost") chose architecture (c): a batch proof verifying 32 reveal proofs, a root proof over one aggregator's batches, and a shrink proof at blowup 2^5 with 56 queries. Its cost figures are modeled from cell counts and a guessed Poseidon2 permutation cost of 400 ns (modeled). This page records what the real layers in proofs/src/layers/ measure, on the machine named below, and says plainly what is not yet established.

Machine: a Lima virtual machine named by its hostname prefix lima- (the rest of the hostname is left off this page), with 16 cores (arm64, CPU implementer 0x61, no model name in /proc/cpuinfo) and 46.9 GiB of memory (measured from /proc/cpuinfo and /proc/meminfo). It is not an aggregator machine of the design; every figure below labelled measured was taken on it and on no other.

## What is built

- <!-- no-figure --> **Batch layer.** A verifier circuit for up to a fixed capacity of child proofs of one AIR and shape, proven as a batch STARK of five tables (wires, arithmetic, Poseidon2 permutation, bits, packing) routed by the LogUp argument of the pinned Plonky3 lookup crate over its batch STARK. It replays each child's transcript, checks its Merkle openings, its FRI folding and its constraints at the out-of-domain point, and exposes its binding, the running digest of the children's ordered public values before and after, and their count.
- <!-- no-figure --> **Root and shrink layers.** The same circuit verifies batch-STARK proofs of the layer below, as the library's verify_batch does: the batch transcript with its lookup challenges, the five tables' constraints and LogUp constraints at the out-of-domain point, the terminal sum, and the opening argument over the randomization, main, quotient, preprocessed and permutation commitments. A root verifies up to a fixed capacity of batch proofs of one key, each starting where the one before ended; a shrink verifies one root proof. Both prove and verify end to end in proofs/tests/layers.rs, and a changed opened row, permutation value, lookup terminal, preprocessed value, folding sibling or quotient chunk of a child proof breaks the circuit.
- <!-- no-figure --> **Lanes.** The wire and arithmetic tables hold several entries a row when a circuit has more wires than the tallest trace the field's two-adic subgroup allows at the layer's blowup; the batch and root take the fewest lanes that fit, the shrink the lanes that make its proof bound smallest.
- <!-- no-figure --> **Binding.** Each layer's transcript absorbs, before any challenge, a tag naming the layer kind, its index, the protocol and statement versions, the chain identifier and the digest of its children's verifier key. That digest is a constant of the circuit, so it sits in the preprocessed columns the layer's verifier key commits to; a root or shrink circuit also recomputes it from the child key's words and absorbs the child's preprocessed commitment in the child's transcript. Every layer's public values carry the binding, and the circuit constrains each child's chain identifier and versions to it: a batch child's from its own public values, a layer child's through its public values and its tag. A real reveal proven on testnet2 and batched under a testnet1 binding is refused by the prover and, proven anyway, by the verifier. Anyone can batch from public reveals: the key is rebuilt from the circuit with no secret of any aggregator. No elliptic curve is used anywhere.

Permutation columns: the width-24 Poseidon2 permutation table has 450 columns a permutation (measured by counting the AIR written in proofs/src/layers/poseidon2.rs), against the modeled 451 of the design, which counted the library AIR's export flag; it is checked against the library's default_babybear_poseidon2_24.

## Poseidon2 permutation

Permutation cost: 897.1 ns per permutation (measured: one scalar width-24 permutation of the pinned library, one core of the machine above, a million permutations, timed by the test time_one_poseidon2_permutation in proofs/tests/layers.rs while no layer proved; repeated runs on the same machine gave 868.7 ns to 1129.3 ns, measured, and the latest run 871.1 ns, measured, so the figure moves by about a quarter from run to run on this virtual machine).

- Against the fleet threshold of 93 ns: missed (measured 897.1 ns, above the modeled 93 ns at which 40 machines suffice).
- Against the merge-budget threshold of 122 ns: missed (measured 897.1 ns, above the modeled 122 ns of the 0.3 s merge budget).
- Against the design's other modeled ceilings: the cost ceiling of 1,226 ns is met by every run (measured 868.7 ns to 1129.3 ns); the delay ceiling of 1,025 ns is met by the 897.1 ns run and missed by the slowest, 1129.3 ns (measured), so it is not yet established on this machine.
- Both thresholds of the design are missed by every run, the fastest included (measured 868.7 ns against 93 ns and 122 ns).

This is a scalar permutation on a virtual machine; the packed permutation a prover uses, and an aggregator core, are not yet established.

## Layers

All three stages are compared with the 128 GiB machine ceiling of the design (modeled ceiling).

Test-shape pipeline (measured on the machine above, by the test a_root_and_a_shrink_prove_an_aggregators_batches): four stand-in children at blowup 2^2 with one query, two batches of two at blowup 2^2 with two queries (tables of 16,384, 2,048, 512, 64 and 512 rows, a proof of 101,138 bytes), a root over both with its wire and arithmetic tables packed into 8 and 2 lanes (tables of 32,768, 32,768, 4,096, 64 and 8,192 rows, a proof of 116,380 bytes), and a shrink of the root (tables of 131,072, 32,768, 4,096, 64 and 4,096 rows, a proof of 107,072 bytes); every proof is within its computed size bound (102,578, 117,136 and 107,900 bytes), and the whole layers test run peaked at 4.66 GiB (VmHWM), far under the 128 GiB ceiling, which is modeled. These runs used the library's own prover, before the streamed prover below; the streamed prover makes the same proofs byte for byte, and its seconds and peak memory at the test shape are not yet established.

Real shapes, planned without proving (measured: the circuits are built for one, two and three slots and their entries counted, by the test the_real_shrink_fits_the_proof_field): a batch of 32 reveals at blowup 2^3 with 96 queries fills 61,964,923 wires and 28,257,959 arithmetic rows, so its tables take 8 and 4 lanes and 8,388,608 rows, the most blowup 2^3 allows; a root over 44 batches fills 75,692,388 wires in 16 lanes of 8,388,608 rows; the shrink of that root fills 1,737,496 wires, packed into 8 lanes of 262,144 rows.

Prover memory: every layer proves with the streamed prover of proofs/src/layers/stream.rs. The library's prover holds every committed matrix (main, permutation trace, quotient chunks, randomization, and the key's preprocessed columns) over its extended domain, sixteen times the zero-knowledge doubled matrix at blowup 2^3, with its Merkle tree and a salt a row, until the last opening. The streamed prover keeps each matrix as its polynomials' coefficients over the doubled domain and evaluates the extension one block of rows at a time, a block at most 2 GiB with its salts and its Merkle subtree (modeled), three times over: to commit, hashing each block into its subtree and keeping only the block roots and the levels above them; to batch the openings; and to answer the queries, evaluating again each block a query falls in, its salts read again from the salt stream at the word they started at, and its subtree rebuilt. The quotient is computed one chunk at a time on the chunk's own coset, the size of the trace. Nothing in the pinned Plonky3 crates is changed: the streamed prover drives their transcripts, Merkle trees, folding and quotient evaluation, so its proofs are the library prover's byte for byte, and the verifier is unchanged. The test the_real_shape_batch_and_root_fit_the_128_gib_ceiling in proofs/tests/layers_memory.rs proves a small circuit of all five tables, one lane a row and packed, with the library's prove_batch and with the streamed prover cut into one block, into many and into blocks of a single row, and requires the same key, the same proof bytes and the verifier's acceptance (measured by that test; the result does not depend on the machine). A root or shrink reads only its child key's table shapes, lookups and commitment, so the child key is used after LayerKey::release_prover_data, which drops the child prover's preprocessed columns and their coefficients, and the benchmark does so before the root and before the shrink. The figures below are its model, PlannedLayer::memory, from the planned tables (modeled); none has been run at the real shape.

Batch: not yet established at the real shape on this machine: its streamed prover needs about 93.3 GiB at its peak (modeled from its planned tables: the circuit 2.7 GiB, the key 13.4 GiB, the committed coefficients 50.3 GiB, the batched openings and the folding layers of the opening argument 24.8 GiB, a block 2.1 GiB), within the 128 GiB ceiling (modeled ceiling) but more than this machine's 46.9 GiB, so the benchmark (proofs/tests/layers_bench.rs, run with -- --ignored --nocapture) prints it as not yet established with that figure instead of running it. Holding every extension whole, as the library's prover does, it needed about 580.5 GiB (modeled). At the test shape a batch of 3 stand-in children proves in about 4.8 s with tables of 32,768, 4,096, 2,048, 64 and 1,024 rows and a proof of 102,840 bytes, and a batch of 32 stand-in children brings the test process to 3.66 GiB (VmHWM) (both measured on the machine above, with the library's prover).

Root: not yet established at the real shape on this machine: a root over 44 batches needs about 116.3 GiB at its peak with the streamed prover (modeled: the circuit 3.4 GiB, the key 20.9 GiB, the committed coefficients 62.9 GiB, the batched openings and the folding layers 25.8 GiB, a block 3.4 GiB), within the 128 GiB ceiling (modeled ceiling) but above this machine's 46.9 GiB, and it needs the 44 batches first. Holding every extension whole it needed about 733.5 GiB (modeled). At the test shape it proves and verifies, as measured above.

Shrink: not yet established in seconds at the real shape on this machine: its streamed prover needs about 8.0 GiB at its peak (modeled), within this machine's 46.9 GiB and the 128 GiB ceiling (modeled ceiling), where holding every extension whole it needed about 78.3 GiB (modeled); it needs the root over 44 batches first, which does not fit this machine. Its proof is at most 1,185,685 bytes at blowup 2^5 with 56 queries, an upper bound computed from its planned tables over the root's real heights and widths (measured by building the circuits; the bound counts every opened value, row, salt, Merkle sibling and length the proof can hold), within the 1,310,720-byte proof field, against the modeled 859,736 of the design. At the test shape the shrink proof is 107,072 bytes (measured).

With the streamed prover every layer of the real pipeline plans within the 128 GiB ceiling at the design's shape, with every layer's blowup and queries, the whole-bundle security bound and the proof field unchanged (modeled); the test the_real_shape_batch_and_root_fit_the_128_gib_ceiling fails if any of them plans above it, and the_real_shrink_fits_the_proof_field and the benchmark print each layer's planned peak. Running the batch, root and shrink at the real shape on an aggregator machine, their seconds and their measured peak memory, are not yet established, and so is the root's verification time against the 3,000 ms budget of spec 07, section 4.
